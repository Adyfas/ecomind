# Why Waste Classification Matters More Than You Think

Every year, over 2 billion tons of municipal solid waste are generated globally — and nearly 40% of it ends up in landfills or open dumps, polluting soil, water, and air. The good news? A simple, daily habit — correct waste classification — can dramatically reduce this crisis.

Whether you're at home, in the office, or on the go, knowing what goes where isn’t just helpful — it’s essential for a livable planet.

# The Four Pillars of Modern Waste Classification

1. 🌱 Organic Waste
What it is: Any material that comes from plants or animals and can decompose naturally.
Examples: Fruit peels, coffee grounds, eggshells, tea bags (without staples), grass clippings, spoiled food.
Where it goes: Compost bin or organic waste collection.
Why it matters: Organic waste in landfills produces methane — a greenhouse gas 25x more potent than CO₂. Composting turns it into nutrient-rich soil instead.

💡 Pro tip: Even small amounts of food scraps matter. Start a countertop compost bin — it’s easier than you think. 

2. ♻️ Recyclable Waste
What it is: Clean, single-material items that can be processed into new products.
Examples: Glass bottles, aluminum cans, clean cardboard, unsoiled paper, plastic bottles (with caps on).
Where it goes: Recycling bin (ensure it’s rinsed and dry!).
Why it matters: Recycling saves up to 95% of the energy needed to produce new materials from raw resources. One recycled aluminum can powers a TV for 3 hours!

⚠️ Common mistake: Greasy pizza boxes, stained napkins, and plastic bags are NOT recyclable — they contaminate entire batches. 

3. ☠️ Hazardous Waste
What it is: Items that are toxic, flammable, reactive, or corrosive.
Examples: Batteries (especially lithium-ion), expired medicines, paint cans, light bulbs (fluorescent/LED), cleaning chemicals.
Where it goes: Special collection points or hazardous waste facilities — never in regular bins.
Why it matters: A single lithium battery can cause fires in waste trucks. Mercury from broken bulbs pollutes water supplies for decades.

🚨 Never throw electronics or chemicals in the trash. Many cities offer free drop-off days — check your local council website. 

4. 🗑️ Residual (Non-Recyclable) Waste
What it is: Mixed, contaminated, or non-recyclable materials that cannot be processed further.
Examples: Dirty diapers, broken ceramics, used tissues, Styrofoam, plastic wrappers, chewing gum.
Where it goes: General waste (landfill or incineration).
Why it matters: This category is shrinking as we become more mindful. The goal? Reduce what ends up here by choosing reusable alternatives.

🌍 Fun fact: If everyone in the U.S. replaced just one plastic bag with a reusable one, we’d save over 100 billion bags annually.
